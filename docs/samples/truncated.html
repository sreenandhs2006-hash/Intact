Limitations

  • A byte-perfect transfer proves only that the backend holds the same bytes the client had. It cannot prove the original recording has no dropped frames.
  • Frames dropped before encoding, with timestamps re-generated at a constant rate, leave no timestamp gap. Only content cues (motion spikes) hint at them, and those are low confidence.
  • Without a capture log or reference recording, capture drops and encoder drops cannot be told apart; such anomalies are reported with likely cause 'undetermined'. With a browser capture log, see capture_evidence.can_prove / cannot_prove for what it settles.
  • Content checks use fixed thresholds on 160x90 grayscale thumbnails. Static scenes, slow motion, fades and scene cuts can cause misses or false alarms. Thresholds are measured in the M3 test set.
  • Only the first video stream is analysed; audio is ignored.
  • Decoder error messages are mapped to frames via packet timestamps; with B-frames the visibly damaged frame can differ from the reported one, and damage can propagate until the next keyframe.
